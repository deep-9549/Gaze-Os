# Ethics application (draft)

**Project title:** GazeOS: webcam-based eye-gaze control of a computer
**Investigators:** [names, roll numbers], supervised by [guide's name], [department, institution]
**Duration:** [start date] to [end date]

## 1. Purpose
To evaluate a software system that lets people control a computer with their eyes using an ordinary
webcam, and to collect a small dataset of normalized eye and face images to improve its accuracy.

## 2. Participants
- 15-20 adults without motor impairments, recruited from [the institution] by notice and word of mouth.
- 2-3 adults with motor impairments (e.g. ALS, spinal-cord injury, cerebral palsy) as case studies, through
  [partner centre], with a caregiver present.
- Exclusions: under 18; photosensitive epilepsy; eye conditions that make screen use uncomfortable.

## 3. Procedure (about 60 minutes, with breaks)
Calibration; pointing task on screen targets; a web-browsing task; typing five short phrases; reading
for five minutes; a short repeat after changing posture. Questionnaires: System Usability Scale and
NASA-TLX. Conditions are counterbalanced with a Latin square. Separate data-collection sessions
(about 15 minutes each, up to three per person) record the participant looking at dots on screen.

## 4. Data collected and storage
- Stored: normalized crops of the face and eyes (224x224 and 36x60 pixels), facial landmark coordinates,
  on-screen target positions, task timings, questionnaire answers, self-reported age group, skin-tone
  group (optional), glasses (yes/no).
- Not stored: full camera frames, names (participants are coded P01, P02...), typed passwords.
- Storage: encrypted drive on [location], accessible only to the investigators; the link between names
  and codes is kept on paper, separately, and destroyed at [date].
- Data is not shared outside the team and never published as images. Aggregate results may be published.
- Personal data is handled under India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (consent, purpose
  limitation, right to erasure).

## 5. Risks and mitigation
Eye strain and fatigue (breaks every 15 minutes, stop at any sign of fatigue); frustration with the
system (participants may skip any task); privacy (as in section 4). The system is not a medical device
and no clinical claims are made.

## 6. Consent and withdrawal
Written informed consent before any recording. Participants may withdraw at any time without giving a
reason; their data is then deleted. For participants with disabilities, sessions run at their pace with
a caregiver present; consent is obtained in the format that suits them (e.g. read aloud, gaze-typed).

## 7. Compensation
[Snacks / small gift voucher of Rs ___], not conditional on completing the session.
